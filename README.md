# Petchefy: clear ownership, better follow-through

A short reading and working guide for Andres, Jeff, and Nan, put together with Daniel's help.

Petchefy needs a shared answer to a basic question: **who is responsible for making each part of the business work?** When that answer is unclear, work gets missed, people duplicate effort, and someone ends up chasing or finishing commitments that belonged elsewhere.

*Traction* by Gino Wickman gives the team a practical starting point. The useful parts explain how to define responsibilities, agree on measurable commitments, and check progress together. They also address a harder question: does the person understand the responsibility, want it, and have the capacity to carry it?

AI can make parts of a job easier. The team still needs someone who owns the result.

## Start here

1. **[Read the Petchefy guide](docs/petchefy-reading-guide.md).** Why these sections matter and how to apply them without turning a small team into a bureaucracy.
2. **[Download the book](books/traction-gino-wickman.epub?raw=true).** Open the EPUB in Apple Books, Kindle, or another EPUB reader. GitHub does not display EPUBs in the browser.
3. **Read pages 99–100, 96–97, and 192–193.** These cover willingness and capacity, defined roles, and checking commitments. Page numbers refer to the original print-page markers embedded in this EPUB; search the section headings if your reader displays different numbers.
4. **[Fill in the working agreement together](templates/team-working-agreement.md).** Use it in a 45-minute team discussion, then review it weekly for four weeks.

The goal of that first discussion is modest: agree on the work that matters, put one owner against each responsibility, and leave with specific commitments for the next week.
